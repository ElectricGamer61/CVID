# 01 · STOP
*Funnel stage 1 — get found, stop the scroll. The first 2–3 seconds (Law 1). Universal hook mechanics live here; the brand's hook lean lives in `BRANDS.md`; output format lives in `scripts/RULES.md`.*

If frame one doesn't stop the thumb, nothing else matters. This is the most leveraged 3 seconds in the engine — go 100% on it and half-ass the rest (Law 3).

**The two hook objects (don't confuse them):**
- **Spoken HOOK** — the RULES.md `HOOK:` line. One full sentence, ≤12 words, sentence case, conversational.
- **Text hook** — beat 1's `On-screen:` overlay. ALL CAPS, ≤7 words, readable in under a second.

**They must be different words** (hook-layering law). Text states one claim; voice complements it. Same words on both channels = one wasted channel. With the visual (motion, product in hand) that's three hooks firing at once — the difference between a scroll-stop and a miss.

---

## THE BUYER-PAIN TEST — who the hook is *for*
A premise-hook stops anyone curious (empty views). A pain-hook stops the person who *has the problem* (a buyer). The test: does frame one indict the **broken thing they currently trust** — the "healthy" choice they're making right now? That's why *"wheat bread is white bread in a costume"* won: it attacks a baseline the viewer trusts *today*. Write for the person with the pain, not the person who's merely interested. (Law 8 applied at frame one.)

## THE 10 HOOK TRIGGERS

Each is a named structure for the first frame. The **rung** column is the `02-HOLD.md` ladder it fires.

**The mix rule:** when brainstorming, generate one **stop-scroll** (1/2/3/6), one **retention** (4/7/8), one **wildcard** — never three flavors of the same trigger. The stop-scroll wins the first second; the retention wins the next eight. **Then only the single winner ships** (RULES.md rule 5) — the trio is internal work, not output.

**Match hooks to format:** a hook that ripped in another niche transports by **noun-swap** — keep the skeleton, swap only the noun ("drop-shipping → Bitcoin → seed oils"). But pull from outliers in the *same format* first; a tier-list hook rarely fits a label-reveal.

## THE DESIRE-BASED HOOK — the one that converts views to signups
The 10 triggers drive *curiosity*; this one drives *desire* — reach for it on `authority`/`invitation` reels. Signal a **dream outcome** delivered by a **relatable, constraint-free character** in the first 2 lines. Desire pulls harder than pain because they finish the picture themselves.

Templates: **About me** ("I [outcome] using [easy method]") · **If I** ("If I wanted [outcome], here's the one thing I'd do") · **To you** ("If you want [outcome], this one swap does it") · **Can you** ("Is it possible to [outcome] with [relatable conditions]?") · **They just did** ("[Someone like you] just [outcome] doing [replicable thing]").

**Kill the constraint.** The second the character needs money, time, or an edge the viewer lacks, they dismiss it. "I dropped the junk without giving up foods I love — one swap" beats "stop eating seed oils."

**One standard deviation:** never say the raw desire ("this will make you healthy") — that trips the BS detector. Hit a concrete *proxy* ("this 'high-protein' bar has more sugar than a Snickers") and let them make the leap. The food-lie callout IS the one-standard-deviation move.

## TWO BORROWED ANGLES — broad hook, conversion payload
*Angle types (not first-frame triggers) for `authority`/`invitation` reels. Pair with a trigger above for frame one.*

- **Cost-of-inaction** — do the 12-month math out loud in their numbers ("skip the swap and that's ~40 lbs of sugar a year you didn't sign up for"). Don't sell; let the gap close the deal.
- **Objection-as-content** — pushback heard 3+ times (comments/DMs) becomes a *broad* video, not a defensive one. Tie the objection to something everyone wants.

## GRADING A HOOK — the pass/fail screen
Every hook (generated or Dennis's draft) gets graded before it ships. **Never ship below B+ — rewrite first.** A soft hook wastes the whole clip behind it.

**Pass requires:**
- Names the broken baseline the buyer trusts today (buyer-pain test)
- Specific, not abstract — a real thing/number beats "a lot" (verified or absent, never invented)
- Declarative and front-loaded — no hedge, no slow question, no run-up; frame one IS the hook
- Opens the loop without spoiling the payoff (rung 4 stays non-obvious)
- One idea only; topic clear in sentence one
- Spoken HOOK ≠ on-screen text

**Auto-fail (the AI-slop tells):**
- **Em-dashes in a hook.** Period or line break instead.
- Vague superlatives ("genius hack," "the best trick") with no concrete detail
- Generic fear kickers ("…or you'll fail") that bolt onto any topic
- Throat-clearing ("so basically," "I want to talk about")
- Generic mystery ("there's something hiding in your X")

When grading Dennis's own draft: give the letter grade, one line per failed rule, then 3 rewrites via the mix rule, then the pick + why.

## WHY IT WORKS (the cognitive backing)
- **Zeigarnik Effect** — the brain can't drop an unfinished thought; an open loop is a nagging open tab. (Curiosity Gap, Story — and the rung-3 stall in `02-HOLD.md`.)
- **Cognitive dissonance** — contradicting a held belief forces engagement to resolve it; agree or argue, both feed the algorithm. (Contrarian, Negation.)
- **Specificity = credibility** — "31 grams" beats "a lot of sugar." Exact numbers read as true — and double as the locked label-read proof beat.
